**Spotting and Managing Dark Personalities**
What the video argues: Dr. Leanne Tenbrinke explains how to identify psychopaths using specific behavioral cues and linguistic patterns. By recognizing these traits early, individuals can establish firm boundaries to prevent manipulation in professional and personal settings.
Key details:
- Psychopaths can be identified in seconds through "Duchenne smiles" (fake smiles with crow's feet) and excessive hand gestures used to control narratives.
- Liars often provide sparse, ambiguous information, making it harder for others to verify their claims or find inconsistencies.
- Research indicates that individuals with high psychopathy often have a less active and less connected prefrontal cortex, impacting impulse control and moral reasoning.
Why it matters: In leadership and high-stakes environments, identifying these traits allows for the implementation of structural safeguards before a manipulator can gain influence over a team or organization.
Source: Diary of a CEO (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcK_zgJjnoo)

**The Death of Mandatory Code Review**
What the video argues: The AMP team has replaced traditional, bureaucratic code review processes with a trust-based model powered by AI agents and "orbs." This shift prioritizes development speed and cloud-based execution over manual human gatekeeping.
Key details:
- "Orbs" allow the team to run applications in cloud-based environments, making local development servers and environments obsolete.
- AI agents now perform comprehensive testing and automation, rendering traditional CI systems redundant.
- The shift relies on a "trust-first" culture where team members have more ownership and "skin in the game" to ensure quality without manual oversight.
Why it matters: This represents a move toward "autonomous engineering," where the bottleneck of human review is removed by reliable AI agents, allowing for massive parallelism in development.
Source: Latent Space (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hvwtHZ5E27c)
