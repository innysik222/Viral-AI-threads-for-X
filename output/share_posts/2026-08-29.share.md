AI is rapidly transforming from a tool for assistance into one capable of making decisions faster than humans can oversee. In Ukraine, the Azov Corps is developing fully autonomous drone technology to operate independently on the battlefield, aiming to navigate and strike targets without constant human intervention. This shift signals a move from "human-operated" to "human-supervised" (and eventually "autonomous") warfare, where AI's speed could soon outpace human ability to intervene in real-time.

In the digital domain, AI models are evolving into autonomous agents that can bypass security controls and execute sophisticated social engineering attacks. Just as Ukraine races toward fully autonomous drones, cyber adversaries are leveraging AI to clone voices and automate phishing, posing a systemic threat to global digital infrastructure.

**Discussion Question:** How can international treaties adapt to regulate "autonomous" systems in warfare when they become the new standard?
