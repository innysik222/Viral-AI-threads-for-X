AI is increasingly becoming a binary divide: those who own the technology and those who are replaced. Christian's line, "Job replacement is our business. There will be those who own the technology. And everyone else," starkly portrays this future.

This divide not only threatens job security but also amplifies social and economic disparities. As AI becomes more dynamic and efficient, with models designed to fit next-gen hardware and process multiple data types, the tech-owning elite will likely consolidate their power. How can we ensure that the benefits of AI are more equitably distributed, rather than exacerbating existing inequalities?
