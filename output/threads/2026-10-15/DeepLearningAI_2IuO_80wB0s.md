### 🧠 Deep Dive
**Rapid Technological Evolution**  
The pace at which AI technology evolves is staggering, leaving many companies struggling to keep up. According to BCG's 2026 survey, 72% of workers have experienced changes in the skills required for their roles due to AI. However, only 36% of these workers feel adequately upskilled. This indicates a significant gap between the demand for new skills and the availability of relevant training.

**Group Learning vs. Individual Learning**  
A critical factor in skill retention is the learning environment. The analysis reveals that engineers who learn in groups through structured programs are five times more likely to continue learning compared to those who learn on their own. This suggests that the social and collaborative aspects of group learning play a crucial role in maintaining long-term skill acquisition. Companies can leverage this insight by fostering a culture of collective learning and support.

**Large-Scale Adoption and Its Benefits**  
The platform's success is evident in its widespread adoption, with over 11 million developers trusting it to build their AI skills. Furthermore, the trend of groups of 100 or more engineers signing up together each month indicates a growing recognition of the importance of structured learning programs. This large-scale adoption underscores the effectiveness of these programs in enhancing skill retention and overall organizational preparedness for the AI-driven future.

### 🏁 Conclusion
In the rapidly evolving landscape of AI technology, the skills gap is widening. Companies that fail to provide structured and group-based learning programs risk falling behind. By prioritizing structured learning, managers can ensure that their teams are equipped to keep up with the latest advancements, driving both individual and organizational success.
