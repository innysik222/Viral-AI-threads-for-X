## 📰 SUMMARY ARTICLE

# 90,000 Rollouts Reveal How AI Agents Do Science - Ian Quigley, Leash Bio (2026-10-27)

**TL;DR:** Leash Bio's Ian Quigley has used advanced AI agents to explore a vast chemical landscape, revealing surprising insights into how smarter models learn to make better decisions and abandon unproductive areas.

### 🔑 Key Insights
*   **Model Performance:** Smarter AI models are better at abandoning unproductive areas, showing a willingness to explore new options.
*   **Scientific Reasoning:** Agents generate plausible medicinal chemistry hypotheses, demonstrating their ability to think like human scientists.
*   **Transparency and Evaluation:** The experiments allow for detailed evaluation of scientific reasoning, providing a new benchmark for AI agents in scientific research.

### 🧠 Deep Dive

#### Agent Performance and Decision-Making

Ian Quigley from Leash Bio has conducted a series of experiments using AI agents to explore a vast chemical landscape, simulating the task of medicinal chemists. The results have shown that smarter models are more adept at abandoning unproductive areas of the search space, while less capable models keep retesting the same molecules. Quigley explains, "More performant models are much better at abandoning the lousy places fast."

#### Scientific Reasoning and Hypothesis Generation

The agents not only find molecules but also generate plausible medicinal chemistry narratives. "Agents will say, 'This ring is good and this particular nitrogen is painful,' and these things are very plausible medicinal chemistry narratives," Quigley notes. This indicates that the agents are internalizing some understanding of the physical nature of molecules and can use this knowledge to generate hypotheses and make decisions.

#### Transparency and Evaluation

One of the most significant aspects of these experiments is the level of transparency they provide. Because the environments are fully measured, researchers can evaluate the performance of the agents in a highly controlled and detailed manner. Quigley states, "We have the capabilities to measure these things at scale, allowing us to understand how agents behave, what mistakes they might make, and how physical feedback influences their learning."

#### Future Implications

The experiments suggest that AI agents can be used to simulate and evaluate scientific reasoning, potentially revolutionizing the way scientific research is conducted. Quigley envisions a future where these environments could be used as a game for human scientists, providing a new tool for training and testing their skills. "This is the same thing that Lyra is doing," he notes, "but they are not doing it live. Here, you can choose almost anything, but you are restricted to the number of choices we made when we pre-measured the environment."

### 💬 Notable Quotes

> "More performant models are much better at abandoning the lousy places fast. And the really poor ones stick around." — Ian Quigley, Leash Bio

### 🏁 Conclusion

Leash Bio's experiments with AI agents in the realm of medicinal chemistry represent a significant step forward in the integration of AI into scientific research. By providing a transparent and scalable environment for experimentation, these experiments offer a new benchmark for evaluating AI agents and their ability to engage in scientific reasoning. As more models are developed and tested, the potential for AI to revolutionize scientific research becomes increasingly clear.
