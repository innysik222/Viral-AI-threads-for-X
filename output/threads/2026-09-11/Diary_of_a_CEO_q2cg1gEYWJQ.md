### 🧠 Deep Dive
**Over-talking and Under-talking:** Vanessa Van Edwards, a likability expert, explains that over-talking is a common issue. According to her research, 95% of people have worked with someone who talks too much. Over-talking can be detrimental to your professional and personal life, as it can prevent you from getting a promotion or meeting your soulmate. 

To test if you're an over-talker, Vanessa suggests asking yourself the following questions:
- Do you leave the conversation realizing you don't know much about the other person?
- Do you notice people nodding politely but not responding?
- Do you love telling long detailed stories, especially when people are too polite to interrupt?
- Have you been told gently or not so gently that you talk a lot?
- While in the middle of a story, do you notice people glancing around the room sometimes?

Balancing your conversation skills is crucial. Vanessa says, "Under-talking is better than over-talking." She explains, "The best conversations are those where you are genuinely curious and interested in the other person. This creates a sense of warmth and connection."

**The "Open Fish" Sign:** Vanessa introduces a non-verbal cue to watch for during conversations. She calls it the "open fish." This occurs when someone opens their mouth as if to say something. If you notice this, it's a signal that it's their turn to speak. Vanessa advises, "If someone is opening their mouth, I call it like the open fish. It means they want to turn. Like you did it to me earlier and I totally stopped."

**Mastering Conversations:** To become a master conversationalist, Vanessa recommends joining her webinar. She provides a blueprint for better conversations and teaches how to avoid awkward silences. She explains, "The key to great conversations or interactions is being interested, not interesting. If you go into a conversation thinking all about yourself, how can I be impressive? How can I be smart and funny? That's okay, but it's quite selfish and it's a lot of pressure. When I started thinking, how can I go into conversation putting this person at ease? How can I make them feel like they can impress me? How can I be interested so to make them interesting? That is a very selfless conversational generosity."
