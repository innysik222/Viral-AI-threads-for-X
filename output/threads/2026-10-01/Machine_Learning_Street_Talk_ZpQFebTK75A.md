# The Future of Formal Verification with Lean: Leo de Moura's Insights

**TL;DR:** Leo de Moura, creator of the formal verification platform Lean, discusses the impact of AI on mathematical proofs, the challenges of community-driven development, and the future of formal verification.

### 🔑 Key Insights
*   **AI Exploits and Security:** Leo reveals that AI has exploited bugs in the Lean system, highlighting the need for a more secure multi-kernel setup.
*   **Community vs. Core:** The development of Lean involves a tension between core system control and community-driven extensions, with the core system now largely managed by a nonprofit.
*   **Scalability and Growth:** Math Lib is expected to grow to 100 million lines, raising questions about how to maintain its scalability and manage the growing size.

### 🧠 Deep Dive
**AI Exploits and Security**
Leo de Moura has highlighted significant security vulnerabilities in the Lean system, particularly in the official kernel and external kernels. These exploits, which he attributes to AI, underscore the need for a more robust and secure system. "Having multiple kernels implemented by different people using different programming languages is a way to accomplish that," he explains. This multi-kernel approach will be crucial to ensure the integrity of mathematical proofs.

**Community vs. Core**
The development of Lean involves a delicate balance between core system control and community-driven extensions. Leo de Moura emphasizes the importance of the "cathedral model" for the core system, where the development is tightly controlled and coordinated. However, the community can develop extensions independently, which adds value and flexibility. "The core system adapts much slower and is controlled by yourself," Leo explains. This model has been successful, but it requires a strong central entity to manage the core development.

**Scalability and Growth**
Math Lib, the library of mathematical proofs in Lean, is expected to grow to an enormous size, potentially reaching 100 million lines. This growth raises significant questions about how to maintain the system's scalability. Leo de Moura suggests that formal verification of the compiler and other components is essential to reduce the trust code base. "We want to prove more things about the kernel, about the code generator," he states. This will help ensure that the system remains reliable and secure.

### 💬 Notable Quotes
> "Having multiple kernels is a way to accomplish that, right? If you have independent kernels implemented by different people using different programming languages, it strengths the situation, right?" — Leo de Moura

### 🏁 Conclusion
The future of formal verification is closely tied to the ongoing development and security of platforms like Lean. As AI continues to evolve, the need for robust and secure systems will only increase. Leo de Moura's insights provide valuable guidance on how to navigate the challenges of community-driven development and maintain the integrity of mathematical proofs.
