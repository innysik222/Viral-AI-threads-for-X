# From Zero to Over a Hundred Robots in 20 Months: Mike LeBlanc of Foundation Future Industries Unveils His Vision for Humanoid Warfare

**TL;DR:** Mike LeBlanc, co-founder of Foundation Future Industries, claims his company can deploy 150 humanoid robots on battlefields within just 20 months. Skeptics remain, but the ambitious timeline and bold claims raise critical questions about the future of military robotics.

### 🔑 Key Insights
*   **Rapid Deployment:** LeBlanc's goal is to have 10,000 robots in operation by year-end, a target that outpaces Tesla’s production goals.
*   **Humanoid Form Factor:** The human form factor offers unparalleled dexterity and adaptability but faces significant technical challenges.
*   **Self-Developed Actuators & AI Models:** Building actuators from scratch is seen as a differentiator, though creating robust AI models remains complex.

### 🧠 Deep Dive
#### Rapid Deployment vs. Skepticism

LeBlanc’s ambitious goal of producing 10,000 robots by the end of the year has drawn skepticism from tech experts and industry watchers. While Foundation Future Industries claims to have deployed over 150 robots in Ukraine for testing, the timeline is still seen as aggressive.

#### Humanoid Form Factor

Mike LeBlanc argues that the human form factor offers unparalleled adaptability and dexterity, making it more suitable than wheeled or robotic dog designs for various tasks. However, this comes with significant challenges, including power supply, strength of actuators, and generalization of AI models.

#### Self-Developed Actuators & AI Models

Foundation Future Industries is building its own actuators from scratch, a move that could give them a competitive edge. However, creating robust AI models to control these robots remains one of the biggest challenges in the industry. LeBlanc emphasizes their focus on specific tasks and the use of video data for training.

#### Deployment Challenges

Deploying robots on battlefields faces significant logistical hurdles. The Ukraine deployment was initially stopped at the Polish border, highlighting the complex bureaucracy involved. Cost is also a concern; while military contracts may be more flexible, sustaining a large-scale operation requires substantial investment.

### 💬 Notable Quotes
> "The human form factor is a species of workaround," says LeBlanc, emphasizing the adaptability of humanoid robots.
>
> "Robots aren't cheap; how many can you afford to lose?" asks LeBlanc. 

### 🏁 Conclusion

Despite the challenges and skepticism, Mike LeBlanc's vision for rapid deployment of humanoid robots on battlefields is clear and ambitious.
