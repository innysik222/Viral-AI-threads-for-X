## 📰 SUMMARY ARTICLE

# AI's Coordination Tax: Why Your Best People Are Burning Out

**TL;DR:** A new survey reveals that for every hour of resolution, businesses spend 3 hours on coordination tasks. This "coordination tax" threatens employee burnout and productivity.

### 🔑 Key Insights
*   **The Coordination Tax:** Businesses spend 3 hours on coordination for every hour of resolution.
*   **Employee Burnout:** Best employees are leaving due to inefficient AI implementations.
*   **AI First Organizations:** Despite higher satisfaction, still face significant coordination issues.

### 🧠 Deep Dive
#### The "Coordination Tax" Defined
Dan O'Connell, CEO of Front, explains that the "coordination tax" is the messy part of coordinating work and sharing context between teams. Businesses are spending disproportionate time managing AI tools and integrating them with existing systems.

#### The Survey Reveals
A survey of 700+ businesses found that the average coordination time is 3 hours for every hour of resolution. This is a significant burden on employees and can lead to burnout. Only 60% of businesses measure this coordination, highlighting the need for better metrics and accountability.

#### The Impact on Employees
According to O'Connell, the best employees want clear problem-solving tools, not AI that creates more work. Companies must ensure AI solutions are designed to drive efficiency and not add to the burden on employees. The survey showed that 40% of businesses do not measure this coordination, making it difficult to identify and address inefficiencies.

#### AI First Organizations
AI-first organizations rate their technology satisfaction highly, but still report significant coordination issues. This suggests that while AI can bring value, it must be implemented with clear measurability and observability to avoid the coordination tax.

### 💬 Notable Quotes
> "The best employees want to have tools that solve clear problems for them and drive efficiency, not more work." — Dan O'Connell, CEO of Front

### 🏁 Conclusion
As AI continues to integrate into business operations, companies must carefully manage how they use these tools to avoid the coordination tax. Failing to do so can result in burnout, decreased productivity, and high turnover rates among top employees. Companies should focus on clear measurability, observability, and efficiency to ensure AI truly benefits their operations.
