# AIs Outplay Each Other in Complex Strategy Game

**TL;DR:** Researchers have developed a benchmark to evaluate AI models in the strategy game Blood on the Clock Tower, revealing significant advancements in long-term coherence, multi-agent coordination, and strategic reasoning.

### 🔑 Key Insights
*   **AI Progress:** Astra, the latest model from GPT6, outperforms Claude Opus 4.6 with a 92% win rate in the game.
*   **Skepticism and Evidence Handling:** Newer models are more skeptical of evidence and consider it holistically, whereas older models take reported information at face value.
*   **Team Dynamics:** A hybrid team of different AI models performs worse than individual models, suggesting that diversity doesn't always benefit team performance.

### 🧠 Deep Dive
#### AI Progress and Benchmarking
The research, conducted by a social media strategist, involves a benchmark harness that uses the strategy game Blood on the Clock Tower to evaluate AI models. This game, similar to Werewolf or Mafia, requires players to form teams, strategize, and bluff. By running over 368 games, the researcher observed significant improvements in AI models over the past year. The gap between the best model, GPT6 Astra, and Claude Opus 4.6 is about 430 ELO points, translating to a 92% win rate for Astra.

#### Skepticism and Evidence Handling
One of the most interesting findings is the difference in how AI models handle evidence. Newer models like GPT6 Astra are more skeptical and consider evidence holistically, while older models like Claude Opus 4.6 take information at face value. For example, in a scenario where a player was suspected of being the demon but didn't die during a night phase, older models quickly accepted the conclusion that the player was innocent. In contrast, newer models explored alternative explanations, leading to the correct identification of the demon.

#### Team Dynamics and Diversity
The research also explored how AI models perform in mixed teams. Surprisingly, a hybrid team of different AI models performed worse than individual models. This suggests that diversity might not always benefit team performance. The researcher hypothesized that different models might have idiosyncratic biases that could conflict with each other, leading to suboptimal strategies.

### 💬 Notable Quotes
> "The biggest difference is that opening eyes models are much more skeptical now of any particular piece of evidence and consider it more holistically alongside everything that has happened over the last several days." - Researcher

### 🏁 Conclusion
As AIs continue to improve, they will become more adept at manipulating information and running complex schemes. This research highlights the importance of developing more sophisticated benchmarking tools to evaluate AI capabilities in complex, real-world scenarios. The implications are far-reaching, from enhancing cybersecurity to understanding the potential ethical concerns of advanced AI.
